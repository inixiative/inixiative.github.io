proposal 01 · multi-scale governance · draft for discussion

No good emperors.

Any system whose performance depends on the virtue of its occupant has no error-correction — it is waiting for its variance to kill it. Rome's Five Good Emperors were an adoption-selection fluke that reverted to Commodus the moment heredity reasserted itself. The design target is an executive that structurally cannot accumulate excessive power, constrained by the layers beneath it — and containment that runs both directions, because the constraint layer is itself where surplus elites lodge and extract.

Signals, not proxies. The system measures terminal values, not instrumental ones: are you happy? Is society fair? Are elites treated the same as everyone else? If you work hard, can you get ahead? These cannot be Goodharted the way GDP can, because the felt experience is the target rather than a stand-in for it. What remains gameable is perception-formation itself — which makes the epistemic environment the load-bearing wall of the entire system.

Counterweighted, not neutral. All power aggregates upward by default. So centralization always requires fresh affirmative consent from the base, and decentralization is the ambient drift. Trust in leaders is designed to be an output of the mechanism — earned through legible incentives and revocable power — never an input it depends on.

plate i · separation of powers by function

Propose · decide · kill

The masses can kill but not draft; delegates can decide but not propose; politicians can propose but not decide. Each tier is incomplete without the others — and the rules for entering and weighting the upper tiers belong exclusively to the lowest one. That is the answer to quis custodiet ipsos custodes: custody of the custodian-rules is held by everyone.

Agenda power is priced, not hidden. Each politician holds a fixed number of use-it-or-lose-it proposal slots per cycle. Agenda-setting theory identifies the proposer as the most powerful actor in any voting system; existing legislatures hide that power in committee chairs and calendars. Here it is explicit, budgeted, and scarce — messaging-bills and legislative spam become irrational.

plate ii · the life of a law

Pay for confirmed durability

Politicians are paid well — for what they pass, and for every year a passed law remains both intact and confirmed by the perception metrics. A law that survives on inertia pays nothing; a law that survives on merit pays for decades. The settling window a politician attaches to their own policy is unpaid, so the only profit-maximizing declaration is an honest estimate of the policy's horizon. And because retention belongs to the masses, someone is always paid to keep making the case for every standing law — nothing survives purely by being forgotten.

“Locked in” means no scheduled poll — never immutable. Past its settling window any law is challengeable at any time, by citizens spending points or delegates spending weight past a modest threshold. Repealing your own obsolete law becomes rational: a dead metric was paying zero anyway.

plate iii · subsidiarity as a discovered quantity

The asymmetric ratchet

The scale of an issue is not decreed constitutionally; it is discovered by the mechanism. Issues that consistently fail to draw engagement at a scale drop to the level below — sustained national indifference reveals an issue was never national. Failure demotes too: when a policy fails its confirmation reviews, jurisdiction drops instead of the failing program receiving more money. Jurisdiction is the rent; competence is the only way to pay it.

An intense minority cannot capture the national scale — their engagement alone cannot clear a national floor. What intensity buys is jurisdiction at their own scale, where their rules bind only themselves: self-governance, not dominion over the indifferent.

Under low civic energy this system does not seize into vetocracy or slide into strongman demand — it decomposes toward smaller scales. Apathy buys smallness, not tyranny.
plate iv · the two axes

Every mechanism sits on two tensions

Consistency — rule of law, precedent, uniform rules — against exploration: local experimentation and policy variance. And the right of exit — leave a polity whose rules you reject — against the right of check: constrain neighbors whose behavior spills onto you. Each mechanism in the design serves an identifiable pole.

known attack surfaces · carried, not papered over

No system stays uncaptured. The design question is the capture rate.

goodhart, managed

Terminal-value metrics remove the proxy gap; gaming relocates to perception-formation and the weight-setting layer. Countermeasures: metric rotation, adversarial red-teaming, keeping the recursive weight-setting layer genuinely contested.

delegation concentration

Every liquid-democracy deployment with data collapsed into power-law super-delegates. Counter-pressure is built in: delegation decay, per-delegate caps, instant revocability, quadratic damping on accumulated weight.

quorum-busting

Strategic abstention killed the Italian referendum system. Answered by approval quorums — silence never counts as a vote — and by counting delegated weight, which makes starving an issue legible and punishable by revocation.

sabotage-to-demote

A faction preferring local control can try to make the national program fail. Demotion requires consistent failure across the backoff schedule, not one bad year — and sabotage of delivery is legible in the perception layer in ways mere underperformance is not.

attribution

Voters punish incumbents for droughts and shark attacks. Mitigations: atomic laws (omnibus bills are attribution-laundering), per-proposal horizons, deliberative panels between raw approval and verdict, and AI as legibility infrastructure — never as verdict-issuer.

the epistemic environment

The cheapest attack on a perception-metric system is manufacturing perception — including manufactured apathy. Three privacy regimes hold the line: anonymous at the ballot, pseudonymous-persistent at delegation, fully public in office.

open decisions · carried explicitly

01The citizen proposal-slot valve — a petition path that mints a slot from below, so the politician class can't become an agenda oligopoly.

02Tally visibility during voting windows — live tallies herd, sealed tallies starve deliberation. Candidate middle: engagement public, direction sealed until lock.

03Who certifies subsidiarity routing — spillover claims need an adjudicator; false claims cost the claimants their points.

04The boundary algorithm's objective function — “the algorithm decides” always means “whoever specifies the objective function decides.” It lives in the meta-rules, on the slowest cadence.

05The enumerated scope-locked portfolio class — explicitly listed, and short.

06The weighting inputs for delegation — deliberately left to the meta-rules layer itself.
