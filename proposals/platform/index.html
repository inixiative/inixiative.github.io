the problem

Fiat quietly taxes everyone who saves. Whoever stands closest to the printer spends new money at old prices; wage earners and savers absorb the difference. The system runs on that transfer.

Crypto solved half of it. Bitcoin is a store of value, and because it appreciates, nobody wants to spend it. Stablecoins move fast, and they import every vagary of the fiat they're pegged to. Savers and spenders ask one instrument to do two incompatible jobs.

Index-fund owners lost their vote. When your savings sit in index funds, a custodian votes your shares. Three firms cast roughly a quarter of the votes across the S&P 500, with other people's money and no accountability to it.

the design: two instruments, one job each

the transaction layer

A currency backed by energy: minted when a kilowatt-hour is produced, burned when it's consumed. Supply grows with real productive capacity, and nobody can expand it to fund a bailout. This is the liquidity layer of the system.

the store of value

A basket of real assets and cash, owned by token holders. Every token is created for cash at its backing value and can be redeemed back to it. Taxes on marketplace activity accrue into the pot: it appreciates as people use the marketplace.

how it works

01tokenize: real assets become tradeable tokens, with rules their own community sets.

02price: sealed-bid auctions prove every price; nobody ever asserts a valuation.

03co-buy: holders vote the basket's purchases, paid in cash it holds; new tokens exist only when someone pays cash at backing price.

04exit: sales pay every owner; the basket swaps the asset for cash, and anyone can leave through the redemption door: tokens burned for cash at backing.

And everything in between sits in escrow. Whenever value is recognized before it's realized (a milestone funded, future capacity pre-sold), it's held against fully-escrowed collateral and released only as delivery verifies.

governance

Your stake is your vote, with no custodian in between. Voting power is the platform tokens you hold plus the value of what you've IPO'd, both counted only at prices strangers paid, never from a self-reported valuation. Large holders still exist, but they vote in the open and answer for it, instead of voting other people's money through a fund's proxy department.

Each project governs itself. Platform-wide votes set platform rules; a project's own token holders govern that project.
