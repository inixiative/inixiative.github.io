the inixiative platform — timeline

Where value is injected, and how it moves.

Four columns, five phases. Every arrow is a real transfer — if a flow isn't on this page, it doesn't exist. The basket is owned collectively by token holders — it holds the cash and the assets: token price = basket ÷ supply. The operator is the company, paid only in fees.

phase 0

Genesis — the basket is seeded

Early believers buy tokens at backing price plus a disclosed fee, in a governance-whitelisted currency. Every backing dollar lands in the basket as cash.

phase 1a

Founding — an inixiative is born

Founders raise in any currency into platform escrow — released only as milestones verify — buy the real asset with it, and tokenize. The platform's service fees are paid in platform tokens.

phase 1b

Trading — the tax engine starts

The inixiative's tokens change hands — the trade itself is just a trade, value moving buyer to seller. Each change of ownership pays a small tax on its value (at the asset's last auction mark, so it can't be dodged by mispricing), and the tax goes into the basket.

phase 2

First IPO — the basket becomes real

IPOs run on a published cycle, announced well in advance. A sealed-bid auction proves the price; the co-buy executes one cycle later, and only if token holders affirmatively vote it — knowing the cleared price. Silence is a no, and you sit out votes on deals you're selling into. The basket pays cash it actually holds; tokens are only ever created at the door, for cash at backing price.

phase 3

The flywheel — usage compounds

More inixiatives, more trades, more IPOs. The tax keeps filling the basket's cash; voted co-buys keep converting that cash into assets. Staggered auction checkpoints keep every mark honest.

phase 4

Exit — supply tracks reality

An asset sells off-platform. Every owner is paid their share. The basket's share buys back tokens on the open market — never above backing — and burns them. Losses pass through the same pipe.

how pricing works

Nothing on the platform is priced by a model, an oracle, or anyone's opinion. Every number is either cash, or a price somebody actually paid.

what how it's priced
an asset sealed-bid auction — at IPO and at periodic checkpoints. The price counts only to the extent unconnected bidders paid it, and it ages: the older the mark, the bigger its haircut, until the next auction re-proves it.
a new token NAV = (cash + every asset at its last auction price, haircut by age) ÷ tokens outstanding. The door sells at NAV + a small spread, paid in a governance-whitelisted currency; if the basket's marks get stale, the door pauses until a checkpoint refreshes them.
a token on the market whatever buyers and sellers agree — it can drift from NAV. Exit buybacks purchase only at or below NAV, so discounts get bought back and burned; premiums are never chased.
every fee, in the open

Every fee is a published, governed parameter. If the platform earns it, it's on this table.

fee charged on goes to

trade tax every change of ownership, at the last auction mark the basket
auction fee running an IPO / checkpoint auction the operator

Portability is priced, not banned. Inixiatives that keep their tokens tradeable only on the platform pay discounted service fees — their trade flow funds the whole system. Inixiatives that choose freely portable tokens pay full fees up front, because their off-platform trades contribute nothing. Either way: the choice is the inixiative's, and the price of it is public.
